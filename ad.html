What is in itA rack that grows with what you want to do

Patch Bay is not one preset behind glass. Every module is yours to add, place and wire, and the rack you build records and exports like any other instrument.

01

72 modules

Ten oscillators, twenty-eight one-shot sound generators, filters, effects, sequencers, a Turing machine, a Karplus-Strong pluck and a talking formant filter.

02

Real patch cables

Drag from a port to a port, or tap one and then the other. Tap a cable to unplug it. Auto-patch rewires the whole rack in one move when you would rather keep playing.

03

Sounds, not just synths

Zap, boom, glass, thunder, cowbell, marimba — one-shot generators retriggered by a key press, so the same rack makes a lead line and a drum kit.

04

Five channels, five racks

Record four parts, each keeping the rack it was made on. Change the rack afterwards and the parts you already played still sound as they did.

05

Sing over it

Channel five records the microphone: your voice on the same clock as the notes, mixed into every export, with a count-in whose clicks never land in the take.

06

Presets and demo tunes

Thirty built-in sounds from acid bass to ambient pad, five demo pieces that each load the rack they were written for, and your own patches kept on the device.

07

A take on the grid

A bar of count-in, a click you never hear in the mix, quantise to the sixteenth, loop, and a knob per channel measured in decibels so the balance is a real one.

08

Files that leave

Mix to WAV or FLAC, or capture OGG. Save a song file and everything comes back — the notes, every channel's rack and the voice take.

09

A visualiser

One button turns the output into a full-screen ring of spectrum, waveform and particles — with a keyboard along the bottom, so you can play it while it runs.

10

Nothing to install

No app store, no account, no download. It is a web page on the Web Audio API, so it runs the same on a phone as on a laptop.

Thirty seconds inThree taps and it is making noise

Browsers will not let a page play a note until it is touched, so the first tap is the beginning — after that it is all yours.

  1. Tap the page to start the audio, then open Demo tunes and pick one. It loads that tune's rack and plays it through the visualiser, so you hear what the synth can do before you build anything.
  2. Wire something up. Open + Add module, add a Delay or a Pluck, then drag from one port to another. Press Auto-patch if the rack goes quiet and let it rewire itself.
  3. Make a track. Pick channel 1, tap Rec, play the keys. Add a part on channel 2, sing on channel 5, then Mix → WAV. The file downloads to the device.

Before you tapWhat it needs, and what it is not

What it needs

A browser that can play Web Audio — Safari, Chrome, Edge or Firefox, on a phone, a tablet or a computer. Headphones or a speaker. Nothing else: no account, no install, no plugin.

The first tap

Browsers will not let a page make a sound before it is touched, so the first tap anywhere starts the audio. After that it plays as you play it, and the page asks the phone for its music volume rather than its quiet one.

Where it is not a studio

It is an instrument, not a DAW: no plugin hosting, no timeline, five channels rather than a console. Nothing is kept between visits except the presets you save on purpose — a song file is how you keep a tune.

Your voice

Channel five asks for the microphone, and only when you tap it. In an app's own in-browser view on iOS the microphone cannot be granted, so the page says so and offers the link to open in Safari instead.

QuestionsThe things people ask first

Is it really free?

Yes. The whole synth — 72 modules, every preset, every export — with no sign-up, no trial and no paid tier. It is open source under the MIT licence, so the source is readable and forkable.

Does it work on my phone?

It is built for one. The rack scrolls, the sheets fit, the keys are sized for a thumb, and the patch view turns itself sideways on a phone held upright so the whole map is the wide picture it was drawn as. Add the page to your Home Screen and it opens with no browser bars at all.

Can I keep what I make?

Mix the five channels down to WAV or FLAC (rendered offline, faster than real time), or capture OGG. Or save a song file — a single .json holding every channel's notes, the rack each channel was recorded with and the voice take — and import it back later, exactly as it was.

Can I use my own samples?

The Sampler loads WAV, FLAC, Ogg Vorbis and Ogg Opus, pitched across the keyboard from its root note, so a sample follows the notes you play. There is also Rec sample: ten seconds of the rack's own output, or of your voice, dropped into a Sampler and playable straight away.

Do I need to know about synthesis?

No. It opens on a working rack that already makes a sound, with an envelope, a filter and a sequencer wired in — and thirty presets and five demo tunes if you would rather start from a sound than from a blank board. Auto-patch does the wiring for you whenever you would rather keep playing.

Is anything sent anywhere?

The page makes one outbound request: an anonymous visitor counter. Every note you play, every take and every file is made on your device and stays there — no audio is uploaded, and there is no account to upload it to. SECURITY.md lists what the page talks to.

Can I host it myself?

It is a folder of static files, so any host that serves https will do: GitHub Pages, itch.io, or a server of your own. No build step, no database, no server code. The README has the steps.
